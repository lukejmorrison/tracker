# Vision

**Totals do not move the needle. Names do.**

tracker.grok.me exists because X’s own transparency tables are real and still not useful to a person who wants to know *what they cannot see in their country*. “69,186 Japanese requests” and “the UK has an Online Safety Act” are the official report’s own weakness. A reader leaves with a number and no picture.

The product is a compiled atlas that answers three questions per country:

1. **Can you load X?** Available, restricted, or blocked.
2. **What, specifically, is restricted?** Named statutes, named duty lists, named category splits — not “illegal content.”
3. **What is in court or at a regulator right now?** A docket with a last-public date, not a vibe.

And a fourth, which we say out loud:

4. **What is still invisible?** Individual withheld posts. Per-country EU and “All others” splits. Anything without a primary source.

## Why this and not another dashboard

Most “censorship trackers” do one of two things: they moralize, or they reprint the official aggregate. Neither changes what a journalist, a lawyer, or a user in Tokyo or Manchester can *do*.

We compile the public layer that already exists and refuse to invent the layer that does not.

- If X published a 96 / 4 split, we show the 96 / 4 split and refuse to invent the 4.
- If X published a 19-item UK duty list, we show the 19 items.
- If Ofcom opened a named investigation, we put the file on the docket with “no findings yet.”
- If the only fact is “folded into All others,” we print that as a gap, not as a guess.

That last move is the product. Honesty about the missing catalog is more useful than a fake completeness bar.

## Editorial bar

A row enters the atlas when it is **named, dated, and sourced**.

| Allowed in | Not allowed in |
| --- | --- |
| A judgment, petition, regulator statement, statute, or X’s own country/duties page | “People say posts disappear in …” |
| A category split X or a ministry published | A 3-way split we inferred from the 96% bucket |
| “No findings yet” on an open file | Treating an investigation as a takedown |
| Volume as context | Volume as proof of political censorship |

Japan is the teaching case. Half of all global legal demands. Almost all of them crime ads — finance, drugs, prostitution. Honor takedowns run on a *different* statutory track with a 7-day clock. Conflating those two is how a tracker becomes propaganda.

The UK is the other teaching case. Removal volume is hidden in All others. The duty list is public. The live file is Ofcom’s Grok investigation. Print the list and the file. Do not invent a UK takedown count.

## What success looks like

A reader in any country can open their page and leave knowing:

- whether X loads
- the **named** things the government or X says are restricted there
- whether a court or regulator file is live *today*
- what this atlas still cannot see

If they only remember a big number, we failed.

## What we will not build

- A scraped feed of withheld tweet IDs. X does not publish it; guessing is worse than a gap.
- A score or “freedom index.” Rankings hide the mechanism.
- Multiplayer, accounts-as-product, or engagement loops. This is a reference work.
- Silent updates. Every compiled date stays on the page.

## North star

The day X publishes a jurisdiction-withheld index, this atlas should be the first place that renders it — country by country, with the same named-duty and docket layers on top.

Until then: compile what is public, name what is missing, and take corrections through the [feedback framework](FEEDBACK.md).
